03 / UCL MSC RESEARCH · JUN–SEP 2026

Efficient Long-Video QA.

My dissertation investigates how to organize long-video information and retrieve evidence on demand, and how those choices affect answer quality, online cost and the value of additional Agent orchestration.

Making Egocentric VQA Efficient · Supervisor: Prof. Gabriel Brostow · Industry collaborator: InferityAI

RESEARCH QUESTION

How should an Agent access video evidence?

01 / PROBLEM

Locating the bottleneck

From a broad research goal, I identified evidence localization and context handling as bottlenecks, then investigated how prepared information changes downstream question answering.

02 / METHOD

Reusable semantic indexing

A training-free hierarchy links events and details to source frames. I compared single-Agent frame inspection with a staged plan–retrieve–verify–answer workflow, and adapted VideoSEAL's retrieval backend.

03 / EVALUATION

Quality and resource use

A stratified 300-question sample from a long-video benchmark supported paired comparisons and ablations of answer correctness, completion and online resource use.

FINDINGS & PUBLIC CODE

Evidence, cost and workflow design.

On a context-feasible paired subset, semantic indexing used about 27% fewer full-workflow online tokens than an object-detection-and-tracking index, with similar observed correctness. Benefits varied with the downstream workflow and evidence budget.

In a supplementary paired comparison, additional staging did not demonstrate a clear answer-quality gain, while the simpler Agent route had lower recorded execution cost. These findings are specific to the tested configurations.

The public repository contains code, experiment records and result provenance. It also provides offline replays and separate instructions for preparing new videos and asking questions; fresh demo outputs are distinct from the recorded thesis results.
