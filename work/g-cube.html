THE STARTING POINT

A business method still needs a technical specification

The source documents described how the company wanted to research a market and reason about business decisions. Turning them into software required identifying the questions, evidence and calculations involved, and understanding how different parts of the analysis depended on one another.

I took responsibility for that translation. As I built the system, I refined a design approach for moving from abstract commercial material to implementable Agent requirements.

The engineering questionWhat has to be made explicit before an Agent can carry out a business method reliably?

MY CONTRIBUTION

From interpreting the brief to building the system

01 / REQUIREMENTS

Make the relationships explicit

I identified dependencies across the business documents and financial models, then used them to define technical requirements. This connected the company's domain knowledge to the system's intended behaviour.

02 / IMPLEMENTATION

Own the technical choices

I selected the technology, designed the Agent and implemented the working demo using LangGraph. The commercial methodology and source material come from Rose's team; the technical design and implementation are my responsibility.

03 / ITERATION

Learn from complete runs

I compare complete outputs and use model-assisted review to assess content coverage and analytical depth. The results inform further development, including where the system spends its effort and what prevents reliable completion.

CURRENT ENGINEERING WORK

Making a useful result more dependable

Evaluation so far has been encouraging on content coverage and analytical depth. Reliable completion remains an active engineering problem: producing a useful report and consistently finishing a long research task are separate things to test.

I am refining resource scheduling and the effort spent on review and revision, with the aim of making long-running jobs finish more reliably. The project remains in development.

Commercial source material, internal workflows, prompts and code remain private. Project discussions and any demonstration materials stay within the company's sharing boundaries.
